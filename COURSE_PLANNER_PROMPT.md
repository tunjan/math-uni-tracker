# Project: Maths Course Planner and Grade Maximiser

## Context
I am a university student taking mathematics courses. Each course has a syllabus, coursework deadlines and exams. The goal of this app is to get me the **best possible grade in every course**. It does that by:
- breaking each course into atomic units of learning;
- scheduling my weeks, days and hours up to each exam using evidence-based study methods;
- testing me systematically, and feeding the results back into the plan.

It is single-user and runs in my browser. It must be **extensible**: every semester I add new courses, and old ones are archived, not deleted.

## Data model (propose the final version in your plan)
- **Hierarchy:** Semester → Course → Topic → Subtopic → Item.
  - An item is atomic: one definition, theorem, technique, example or exercise type.
  - IDs are permanent and namespaced by course, e.g. `MA2001:GR.07.2`.
  - Progress always references IDs. Records whose IDs disappear are listed in a warning panel and never deleted, as in the old app.
- **Per course:**
  - Title, code, credits.
  - Assessments: exams and coursework, each with a date or deadline, weight in the final grade, format, duration, and whether a calculator or notes are allowed.
  - My target grade.
- **Per item:** the fields from the old app (dateStarted, dateFinished, 0–5 confidence with unrated ≠ 0, notes with inline LaTeX), plus:
  - `estMinutes`: an AI estimate I can edit.
  - `examWeight`: how often it appears in past papers.
  - `difficulty`.
- **Per subtopic:** books, documents, test attempts and status, as now.
- **Documents:**
  - Files belong to a course, and optionally to a topic, subtopic or assessment.
  - Kind: syllabus, past paper, mark scheme, lecture notes, problem set, solutions, my working, AI feedback.
  - Source: from class, generated by AI, or written by me.
  - Each records its name, size and date added. Stored as blobs in IndexedDB.
- **Study sessions:** planned and actual. Each has a date, start, duration, the items it covers, and a type: learn, practise, retrieval test, review, mock exam or buffer.

## Course setup from PDFs (AI)
When I create a course I upload the **syllabus and past papers** as PDFs. The app sends them to the OpenRouter model, which returns a proposed course structure as strict JSON, validated by Zod:
- topics, subtopics and atomic items;
- prerequisites;
- an estimated study time per item;
- a per-item exam-frequency weight inferred from the past papers;
- assessments, with any dates it finds.

Show me a **preview** with the counts and the full tree, let me edit it, and only save once I confirm. Invalid JSON is retried once and then shown to me, never saved. Re-running setup later produces a diff against the existing course, so IDs and progress are never lost.

## Planning: organise my weeks, days and hours (core feature)
**Inputs:**
- Assessments and deadlines.
- My weekly availability: hours per weekday plus time windows.
- Blocked dates.
- A daily cap.
- Session length preferences.
- Progress, confidence, test results and AI grades.

**Output:** a schedule from today until each exam, made of study sessions, shown three ways:
- **Today:** a checklist of sessions.
- **Week:** a calendar grid.
- **Course timeline.**

**How it must plan:**
- **Code does the allocation, not the model.** Build the scheduler as a deterministic, unit-tested TypeScript module. The model supplies estimates; the scheduler does the allocation, so it is reproducible and explainable.
- **Learning order:** respect prerequisites. For each item, learn it, then do worked examples, then practise problems, then a retrieval self-test.
- **Spaced repetition** of every learned item at expanding intervals, roughly 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days, adjusted by confidence and test results.
- **Interleave** topics in practice sessions once their basics are done.
- **Prioritise** by assessment weight × exam frequency × weakness (low confidence, failed tests, low AI marks), against time remaining.
- **Before each exam:** timed mock exams built from past papers and AI-generated papers, a review session after each, and buffer days. Nothing new in the last days unless I'm behind.
- **Replanning:** when I miss or overrun a session, or a grade comes back low, offer a "Replan" button. It reschedules from today and shows what moved. Never silently rewrite the past.
- **Feasibility check:** if the plan doesn't fit, say so plainly ("You need 41h for MA2001 before 12 Jan, but have 28h available"). Suggest what to cut or where to add hours.
- **Plan vs actual:** track it and show it on the dashboard.
- **Calendar export:** export the plan as `.ics`.

## Copy-paste prompt generator
At the subtopic and item level, and for a set of selected items, provide buttons that build prompts I paste into another AI chat:
1. **"Copy problem-set prompt".** Practice problems in increasing difficulty: warm-up, standard, exam-level, challenge.
   - The prompt must tell the AI to use its **math problem set generation skill** if it has one, and also contain the full requirements so it works without that skill.
   - **Requirements:**
     - LaTeX math.
     - Problems in the style and level of my past papers; include a short excerpt or description of the relevant past-paper questions.
     - Separate full worked solutions.
     - A marks allocation per question.
     - A machine-readable **mark scheme** in a fenced ```json block, in a schema you define, so I can paste it back into the app.
     - An optional timed **mock-exam** variant that matches the real exam's format and duration.
2. **"Copy study-notes prompt".** Rigorous study notes that:
   - expound the topic thoroughly and in a motivating way;
   - never skip an algebraic step or a step in the logic;
   - state every definition and theorem precisely;
   - give intuition before formalism;
   - include worked examples and common mistakes;
   - end with a self-test.

**Every generated prompt includes:**
- The course and level.
- The exact item list, with IDs and titles.
- The prerequisites I already know.
- My weak points from test attempts and AI feedback.
- The output format I want.

Show a preview before copying. Keep the templates in one editable file so I can tune them.

## AI grading of my mock exams and problem sets
1. Upload my handwritten working as photos or a PDF. Attach the paper and its mark scheme: either the pasted JSON from the generated prompt, or an uploaded past paper with its official scheme.
2. The OpenRouter model, which must support vision, grades each question against the mark scheme. It returns strict JSON, validated with Zod:
   - marks awarded out of available, per question and part;
   - where the working went wrong;
   - missing justification;
   - the items each question tests;
   - an overall percentage and estimated grade band.
3. Show the feedback with math rendered. I can **override any mark**, and the result is marked "AI-graded, reviewed by me".
4. Save the working, the feedback and the result as documents.
5. Feed the results back:
   - Low marks lower confidence on the tested items and add review sessions to the plan.
   - A graded mock counts as a test attempt for its subtopics.
   - Keep the old status rules: a pass is a score of at least 4 out of 5, on or after the last item finish. Map percentages to 0–5 in a rule you state.
6. Be honest in the UI that AI grades are estimates.

## OpenRouter integration
- **Calls:** call `https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/chat/completions` directly from the browser; it is OpenAI-compatible.
  - **Check current OpenRouter docs** for PDF input (`file` parts and PDF parsing options), image input, structured outputs (`response_format` with a JSON schema) and returned usage/cost before relying on them.
- **Key storage:** a Settings page where I paste my OpenRouter API key, stored only in IndexedDB on this device.
  - Never include it in exports, URLs or logs.
  - Tell me to set a spending limit on the key in OpenRouter.
- **Models:** load the list from OpenRouter's models endpoint. Let me choose separate models for course setup (long context + PDF), grading (vision + strong reasoning) and in-app note generation. Never hardcode a model.
- **Cost:** show the token usage and cost of each call, and a running total per course.
- **Errors:** handle them cleanly: bad key, rate limit, model without vision, malformed JSON. Never lose my uploaded files when a call fails.
